#### Cipher Progress Reduction

(Please read this part carefully, especially if you’re new)  
During the time the Camera World is active:

- If a cipher gets decoded from 0% to 50% , the cipher will be at 25% after it collapses.
- If a cipher that is already at 20% (before Camera World activates) and then gets decoded to 60%, the cipher will be at 40% (not 30%) after it collapses.
- If two survivors are decoding the same machine at the same time from 0% to 50%, but one is in the real world and the other in the Camera World, the cipher will remain as 50% after it collapses.
- If Abnormal is used on an 80% decoded cipher, reducing it to 48%, the cipher will be at 64% after it collapses.  
    Abnormal takes off 60% of 80 = 48%;  
    Progress difference between Camera World and real world = 80 - 48 = 32;  
    Half of difference merging into the real world = 32 / 2 + 48 = 64

A common misconception many people believe in is that Joseph counters cipher rush — he does not. An already 90% decoded cipher will stay as 90% no matter how many times Camera World is activated afterwards. 

In other words, the Camera World will reduce the cipher progress by half the difference between the real world and the Camera World.

This is why Joseph’s matches tend to be longer, because the Camera World prolongs the game.


#### Camera World Damage

The projected damage it inflicts is also based on half the HP’s difference between the real world and the Camera World, but ONLY if the mirror image has more damage than the real survivor.

General concept if the survivor is at full health:

- 1.0 damage on a mirror image → 0.5 damage to the real survivor
- 1.5 damage on a mirror image → 0.75 damage to the real survivor
- Knocked down mirror image (equivalent to 2.0 damage)  → 1.0 damage to the real survivor

Mirror image placed on a rocket chair (equivalent to 4.0 damage) → knocked down state on the real survivor
